# 🀄 Riichi Realm — Mahjong Scorecraft Trainer

> *Where tiles whisper, winds turn, and every discard tells a story.*

Riichi Realm is an immersive, browser-first training ground for players who want to read the table the way a novelist reads a page — not just count points, but feel the rhythm of the round. Built as a spiritual successor to score-tracking utilities, this project reimagines what a mahjong trainer can be: part dojo, part ledger, part companion that grows alongside your intuition.

Whether you are a fledgling riichi enthusiast learning why a closed hand with a single wait can flip an entire match, or a seasoned club player chasing that elusive yakuman, Riichi Realm provides the scaffolding, the feedback loops, and the calm interface that lets your decision-making mature.

## 🌱 Why Riichi Realm Exists

Mahjong scoring is deceptively simple on the surface — han, fu, a multiplier here, a bonus tile there. But beneath the arithmetic lies a language. A language of patience, probability, and psychological warfare waged between four players and one hundred and thirty-six beautiful little rectangles.

Riichi Realm began as a quiet rebellion against score calculators that treated players like spreadsheets. It became a place where:

- **Learning is gradual** — difficulty curves that respect your pace.
- **Feedback is immediate** — every discard, every call, every riichi gets contextual commentary.
- **Style is personal** — track your tendencies across sessions and watch your identity as a player emerge.

The original inspiration — a community score trainer hosted under a horned domain — proved that players wanted more than a calculator. Riichi Realm takes that seed and grows it into a garden.

## ✨ Feature Constellation

Every feature in Riichi Realm is a star in a larger constellation, each one positioned to help you navigate the darkness of an unclear hand.

### 🧮 Scorecraft Engine
- Real-time han and fu computation with annotated breakdowns.
- Support for closed and open hands, including situational yaku like *menzen tsumo*, *chankan*, and *haitei*.
- Visual explanation of every point source, so you learn *why*, not just *what*.

### 🎴 Hand Trainer Studio
- Thousands of curated scenarios, from beginner-friendly waits to nightmare multi-sided riichi traps.
- Adaptive difficulty that reads your error patterns and pushes you exactly where you are weakest.
- Timed drills and untimed study modes, each with their own vibe.

### 🧠 Decision Journal
- Log your reasoning on pivotal discards, then revisit it after the round resolves.
- Compare your instincts against suggested alternatives — not to shame you, but to sharpen you.
- Export your journal as a portable record for coaching sessions.

### 📊 Progress Analytics
- Win rate, deal-in rate, riichi efficiency, and dozens of other metrics, all charted over time.
- Session heatmaps that reveal when you play sharpest.
- Trend lines that celebrate improvement without ever becoming a leaderboard.

### 🕰️ Replay Theater
- Revisit any session and step through it tile by tile.
- Overlay annotations, pause at critical junctures, and compare alternate lines.
- Slow-motion the moment you chose to push — and the moment you should not have.

### 🌐 Table Ethics Notes
- Gentle, non-preachy reminders about etiquette and sportsmanship.
- Cultural context for players new to Japanese mahjong traditions.

## 🎨 Design Philosophy

Riichi Realm treats the interface the way a tea master treats a room: nothing extra, nothing missing.

- **Calm by default** — muted greens, warm paper tones, and an absence of flashing urgency.
- **Motion with meaning** — tiles that slide, never teleport; numbers that count up, never snap.
- **Focus preservation** — no notifications, no streaks that guilt you, no dark patterns.

The metaphor is the **engawa**, the veranda of a traditional Japanese home: a place that sits between the noise of the outer world and the quiet of the inner one. Riichi Realm tries to be that veranda for your mahjong practice.

## 🧭 Screens & Modules

| Module | Purpose | Typical Session Length |
|---|---|---|
| Score Workshop | Compute, dissect, learn | 5–15 minutes |
| Drill Arena | Rapid-fire scenario practice | 10–20 minutes |
| Journal Room | Reflect and annotate | 15–30 minutes |
| Analytics Observatory | Review trends and patterns | 5–10 minutes |
| Replay Theater | Rewatch and reconsider | As long as curiosity lasts |
| Glossary Grove | Lookup yaku, terms, and etiquette | 2–5 minutes |

Each module links gracefully to the next — a drill session can spawn a journal entry, which can be replayed in the theater, which surfaces an analytics insight that sends you back to the workshop. The loop is a spiral, not a treadmill.

## 🌍 Multilingual Reach

Riichi is played from Sapporo to São Paulo, and Riichi Realm speaks to that world.

- Interface localization for Japanese, English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Korean, Portuguese, French, Spanish, and German.
- Terminology modes: keep *riichi*, *tsumo*, and *ron* in their native form, or toggle translated glosses if you are still building vocabulary.
- Directional reading support for future RTL expansion.
- Community-sourced translation suggestions are welcomed with open arms.

The goal is not merely translation — it is **transplantation**, so a term lands in your mind with the same weight it carries at a Tokyo table.

## 📱 Responsive Interface

A mahjong hand tells you everything in a single glance. The interface should behave the same way.

- **Desktop** — full canvas, tile animation, side-by-side panels for teaching moments.
- **Tablet** — touch-optimized tile selection with generous hit targets and haptic-like feedback cues.
- **Mobile** — single-column focus modes, swipe navigation, and a score keypad that never obscures the hand.
- **Handheld consoles & e-readers** — a text-first fallback mode for players who want to study on the quietest devices.

No matter the screen, the spirit stays constant: clarity without clutter.

## 💬 Support & Community

You are never alone at the table — even in a solo drill, the design remembers that learning is social.

- **Round-the-clock help desk** — inquiries are answered at any hour, every day of the year, because mahjong players are famously nocturnal.
- **Discussion channels** organized by skill band, so beginners are not drowned out by veterans (and veterans are not bored by beginners).
- **Office hours** hosted regularly for structured Q&A on hand reading, scoring edge cases, and etiquette questions.
- **Feature requests** are read by humans, weighed with care, and frequently ship.

Support here is not a ticket queue. It is a hand extended across the table.

## 🔧 Technical Landscape

Riichi Realm is built to be a long-lived companion, not a flash in the pan.

- **Client-first architecture** — the interface runs comfortably in modern browsers without needing a persistent connection for core study features.
- **Portable state** — your journal, drills, and analytics can be carried between devices through export and import.
- **Deterministic scoring core** — the same hand always produces the same result, with a full audit trail.
- **Accessible from the ground up** — keyboard navigation, screen-reader landmarks, and color-blind-safe palettes.
- **Offline-friendly** — core study modes continue to work even when your connection drifts away.

No installation incantations required. Open the project, and it begins.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Riichi Realm meant for absolute beginners?**
Yes. The earliest drills assume nothing beyond curiosity, and progress is ungated — you move forward when you feel ready, not when a meter tells you to.

**Do I need prior experience with Japanese mahjong specifically?**
Not at all. If you have played any mahjong variant, the glossary and introductory scenarios will help you bridge the gap. If you have not, you will still find a gentle on-ramp.

**Does the project store personal data?**
Study progress lives with you. Analytics are computed locally unless you explicitly choose to share them for feedback.

**Can I use this alongside a club or study group?**
Absolutely. The journal and replay modules are designed to be shared when you want to be, and private when you do not.

**Does support really run around the clock?**
Yes — questions are answered at all hours, every day of the year. Some of the best conversations happen at 3 a.m.
